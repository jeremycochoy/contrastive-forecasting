# RevEWMNorm span search on ARIMA data

**Question.** When the contrastive backbone is trained on non-stationary
ARIMA(1,*p*,*q*) data, what input-normalization `span` does it want — and does
it need normalization at all?

> *RevEWMNorm = reversible exponential-weighted-moving-average normalization on
> the model input. `span` sets how fast the EMA adapts (pandas convention:
> α = 2/(span+1)); smaller span = faster adaptation = shorter memory. We
> sweep span on a log grid plus a no-normalization baseline.*
>
> *Contrastive gap = FF − FP: how much more a window's forecast resembles its
> own future (FF) than its present (FP) — the margin the contrastive loss
> grows. Higher is better; ~0 means the backbone learned nothing useful.*

## Result

**Normalization is essential** — the one large, robust effect. Within the working
spans the gap forms a **broad plateau: span 16–91 all clear ≥10× the no-norm 0.020
baseline**. The **best-observed region is sub-patch (16–32, ~0.23, vs ~0.21 for span
45–91)**, but at one run per span neither that separation (~0.018) nor the 32-vs-16
ordering (0.235 vs 0.228) is firmly outside single-seed noise — so use *a* sub-patch
span, but don't read the within-plateau ranking as established.

Highest at span=32 (0.235), with span=16 a single-seed near-tie (0.228), then an uneven decline (a small span=45→64 wiggle, then a steeper fall past span=91) as the EMA adapts too slowly. The curve is single-peaked and shallow on top. Everything from span=16 to
span=91 clears 0.21 (10–12× baseline); only the long-memory tail (span=128 →
512) falls off meaningfully as the EMA stops tracking the drift. The
sweet-spot half-life is **sub-patch** — span=16/32 correspond to 0.4/0.7
patches — and the two half-life-=-1-patch / =-2-patch points (span=45, 91)
both land at ~0.21, *below* the sub-patch pair. The two extremes fail in
opposite ways: **span=8 diverges to NaN** (a numerical overflow in the EMA
cumsum at T=4096 — not variance collapse; see *What we learned*; confirmed in
`logs/span_8.log`), and **span=512 under-adapts** (gap 0.132).

The per-step trajectories that this best-gap curve summarizes:

![Contrastive gap vs training step, one line per span (single run each). The normalized runs rise over 3k steps — not strictly monotone (e.g. span=64 dips at 2.5k before recovering); the no-norm baseline (grey dashed) barely lifts off zero. span=8 (red dotted) is NaN from the first validation. No-norm sits flat near the x-axis the entire run — the non-stationarity of
integrated ARIMA (drifting mean, growing variance) makes the raw input nearly
unlearnable for the contrastive objective. Among the normalized spans the
lines stay tightly bunched but the within-plateau *order keeps reshuffling*
through 3k (span=16 climbs from last at 1.5k to 2nd by 3k; span=45 slips from
1st to 4th), which is exactly why the span=16-vs-32 ordering should be read as a
single-seed near-tie, not a decisive win (see caveat below).

## Protocol

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Backbone | Tiny: C=4, H=512, L=6, W=16, GRU encoder, 8 heads (19,952,384 params) |
| Data | ARIMA(1, *p*, *q*), *p,q* ∈ {1..8} (dimension=8), generated per batch |
| Optimizer | AdamW, lr 1e-4, batch size 8 |
| Steps | 3,000, validating every 500 on a fixed seed-0 ARIMA batch |
| Hardware | 2× RTX 4090, ~4.5 GB VRAM/run, two runs/GPU |
| Spans swept | 8, 16, 32, 45, 64, 91, 128, 512, and None (no-norm) |

**Single-seed sweep — one run per span, no seed replication, no error bars.**
Differences inside the flat top of the curve (notably span=16 0.228 vs span=32
0.235, a 0.0077 gap) are within plausible single-run noise and are *not*
established as real — the whole span 16–91 plateau is statistically
indistinguishable here. span=32 is the best *observed* value and the project's
operating default, but this sweep does not establish it over span=16. The figures are
generated by `scripts/plot_span_sweep.py`, which parses the committed
`logs/span_*.log` directly (the authoritative source); the training driver is
`scripts/span_search.py`.

## What we learned

- **RevEWMNorm is required, not optional, for integrated ARIMA input.** No-norm
  reaches gap 0.020 in 3k steps; any working span reaches 0.13–0.24. This is
  the one large, unambiguous effect in the sweep.
- **The backbone prefers a sub-patch EMA half-life (≈0.4–0.7 patches, span
  16–32).** The optimum is a broad plateau, not a sharp point — span 16→91 all
  clear 0.21, and at single seed the 16-vs-32 difference (0.228 vs 0.235) is
  within noise — so the supported recommendation is *"use a sub-patch span"*,
  not *"use exactly 32"*. span=32 is the best *observed* value (0.235) and the
  project's operating default; **span=16** (0.228) is equally defensible and
  sits further from the span=8 divergence cliff.
- **The two failure modes are different.** span=8 NaNs from a *numerical*
  overflow: the EMA cumsum's `inv_decay = 1/decay` blows up once `decay=(1−α)^t`
  underflows to 0 (at t≈2961 for span=8, *inside* the T=4096 sequence — the
  code's own NOTE in `src/norm.py` warns of it); span=16 survives only because
  its underflow threshold (t≈5947) sits past T=4096. span=512 under-performs for
  a separate, *bandwidth* reason — the EMA can't track the drift. (The optimizer
  was never swept, so an HP interaction is not ruled out at the span=512 end.)

*Single-seed caveat applies throughout: the plateau ordering (16/32/45/64/91)
could reshuffle under seed replication; only the norm-vs-no-norm gap and the
two extreme-span failures (span=8 NaN, span=512 under-adapt) are robust to it.*

*(Operational detail — round layout, the span=128/512 log-buffering ticks, the
span=8 NaN mechanism — in [notes/execution-log.md](notes/execution-log.md).)*
