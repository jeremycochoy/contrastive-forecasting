\begin{abstract}
  Current time-series foundation models forecast in value space,
  following the language-modelling recipe of next-token prediction.
  Because the observed series is one realisation of a stochastic
  process, a value-space loss asks the model to reproduce intrinsic
  noise that no model can predict. We propose \emph{Contrastive
  Forecasting}, a training framework in which a patch embedding, an
  encoder and a causal forecaster are trained jointly to predict the
  next patch in latent space. The forecasts align with the latents of
  an EMA teacher, a contrastive loss separates the latents of
  different patches and series, and the SIGReg regulariser prevents
  the collapse of the latents. The framework is decoupled from the
  choice of encoder and forecaster architecture. A small head, trained
  on the frozen model, maps the latent forecasts back to values. We
  train and evaluate on GIFT-Eval. We demonstrate the feasibility of
  training a forecaster in latent space only. The downstream forecast
  in value space keeps most of the performance of a value-space model
  trained with the rest of our recipe. After training on $18.5\%$ of the
  GIFT-Eval pretraining corpus, our model scores a GM-MASE of
  $0.9401$ with $15.0$ million parameters, in line with a
  Moirai-inspired value-space model of the same width, which scores
  $0.9362$ with $14.4$ million parameters.
\end{abstract}
