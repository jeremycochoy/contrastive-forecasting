\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

The foundation models of Section~\ref{sec:introduction} compute their
loss on the values of the series. Two earlier lines of work move the
prediction of the future into a latent space: Contrastive Predictive
Coding and the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture.

Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC)~\citep{oord2018cpc} encodes each
step of a signal with a strided convolutional network, and a GRU
summarises the past latents into a context vector. For each horizon
$k$, a linear map of the context predicts the latent of the step
$t + k$. The InfoNCE loss~\citep{oord2018cpc} trains the model to
find the true future latent among negative latents, sampled from other
times and other sequences. This loss is a lower bound on the mutual
information between the context and the future, so the encoder keeps
the part of the past that predicts the future. CPC learns
representations for speech, images, text and reinforcement learning.
In speech and vision, the evaluation trains a linear classifier on the
frozen representations, for example to recognise phones or speakers.
CPC does not try to forecast. No part of the model maps a predicted
latent back to the values of the signal, and the paper reports no
forecast error. The time-series successors keep the contrastive loss
and change the positive pairs: a subseries and a shorter subseries
inside it in T-Loss~\citep{franceschi2019tloss}, temporal
neighbourhoods in TNC~\citep{tonekaboni2021tnc}, and two augmented
views of the same time step in TS2Vec~\citep{yue2022ts2vec}. These
methods target classification and anomaly detection. TS2Vec also
reports forecasts, from a ridge regression that it fits on the frozen
representations of each dataset.

The Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture
(JEPA)~\citep{assran2023ijepa} comes from vision. A context encoder
reads the visible part of an image. A target encoder reads the full
image, and its latents of the masked blocks are the targets. The
weights of the target encoder are an exponential moving average (EMA)
of the weights of the context encoder. A predictor maps the context
latents to the target latents with an $L^2$ loss. JEPA uses no
negatives. The stop-gradient on the target branch and the asymmetric
predictor prevent the collapse to a constant, which is a global
minimum of the loss. TS-JEPA~\citep{ennadir2025tsjepa} applies this
recipe to time series. It cuts each series into patches, masks
$70\%$ of them, and trains with an $L^1$ loss in latent space. It
trains one model for each dataset and evaluates it on classification
and on forecasting. In its short-term forecast experiment, the
autoregressive baseline of the paper has a lower error than TS-JEPA on
two of the three datasets.
LaT-PFN~\citep{verdenius2024latpfn} combines JEPA with a prior-data
fitted network for zero-shot forecasting in context.
LeJEPA~\citep{balestriero2025lejepa} removes the EMA teacher and the
stop-gradient. It adds SIGReg, a regulariser that pushes the
distribution of the embeddings to an isotropic Gaussian, and this
regulariser prevents the collapse.


Our model takes from each of these works. It forecasts the next patch
with a causal transformer, as TimesFM~\citep{das2024timesfm} does. Its
forecast is a latent, as in CPC and JEPA, and it must match an EMA
target, as in JEPA. A contrastive loss with negatives, as in CPC, and
the SIGReg regulariser of LeJEPA keep the latents away from collapse.
Unlike CPC and TS-JEPA, it trains one model on many datasets for
forecasting, and we score it with a forecast error on the GIFT-Eval
benchmark~\citep{aksu2024gifteval}.
