\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We follow the next-token objective of language models: our model
predicts the next patch of a series. Following JEPA, it predicts the
latent of that patch rather than its raw values, so it does not have to
reproduce the noise of the series. A contrastive loss with negatives
makes the latents of different patches and series stay apart, so the
latents keep as much information about the series as they can, and
SIGReg keeps them from collapsing (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
The whole
model trains without labels. To show that its latent forecasts hold the
information of the future values, and that this information is easy to
read, we freeze the model and train only a small head that maps the
latent forecasts back to values.

On GIFT-Eval (Section~\ref{sec:results}), our model forecasts as well
as a value-space model trained with the same data and the same budget,
at both widths. The task is harder for it: its backbone never sees a
loss on the values, and only the head, trained afterwards, reads
the values out of the latent forecasts. We take this result as evidence
that forecasting in latent space is a viable way to train a time-series
foundation model. The ablations of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} will
measure the effect of each part of our recipe.

The evaluation is still narrow. A latent space that keeps the information
of the series should also serve other tasks, such as the
classification of series and anomaly detection. Each task needs only
a small head on the frozen model, so the same backbone can be scored
on all of them. We have also not tried to fine-tune the backbone on a task, which
may score better than a head on a frozen model.

One limit of the method is that its training loss does not tell how
good the model is. A low loss in latent space can still hide a poor
representation. To judge a model, we must train the head and score the
forecasts on a benchmark, which costs time at each save. A metric in
latent space that predicts the forecast error would make the iteration
loop much faster.
